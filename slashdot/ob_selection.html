01

Preamble

Slashdot, as the programming and designing club of a research institute, occupies a niche position within the broader student community at IISER Kolkata, where a significant fraction of the student body does not necessarily engage with programming, coding, or computational design on a regular basis. While we firmly believe in the principles of democratic election, a purely popularity-driven voting mechanism risks electing individuals who may lack the foundational competence and sustained interest that the role of an Office Bearer demands.

This proposal therefore outlines a structured, transparent, and merit-sensitive evaluation process that balances democratic participation with domain-relevant scrutiny, ensuring that the next generation of OBs is simultaneously interested, responsible, and knowledgeable.

03

Minimum Candidate Expectations

While it is not strictly necessary for an OB candidate to be an expert programmer or designer, that framing would be a gross understatement of the competence threshold we expect. These are not gatekeeping measures meant to exclude newcomers; they are baseline indicators that a prospective OB possesses enough context to lead, organize, and represent a programming and designing club credibly.

✓  A working understanding of how programs are written, compiled, and executed, well beyond the level of simply clicking "Run" in an IDE.

✓  Familiarity with foundational concepts of how a computer functions under the hood, including a basic grasp of operating systems, particularly Linux-based environments.

✓  Awareness of different programming languages, their paradigms, and their respective use cases, without necessarily having mastery over all of them.

✓  Appreciation of designing philosophies, whether in UI/UX, graphic design, or computational and algorithmic design, depending on the candidate's area of interest.

★  A genuine and demonstrable interest in the club's mission, which remains the single most important criterion above all others.

04

Evaluation Tracks

The evaluation process offers two tracks. A candidate will follow one of the two, and the outcome of whichever track they pursue will feed directly into the final election. Regardless of the track chosen, every candidate will be expected to present a manifesto outlining their vision and plans for the club during the election.

TRACK A

The Interview

Part 1: Candidate Presentation

Each candidate will deliver a presentation covering their vision and concrete plans for the upcoming session, specific areas where they believe Slashdot currently falls short along with proposed remedies, what distinguishes them from other candidates and why the panel should vote in their favour, and their current skill set, projects, and relevant experience.

Part 2: Question & Answer Session

Following the presentation, questions will be posed probing the depth and breadth of the candidate's current technical skills, their understanding of club operations and community building, and their ability to think on their feet when confronted with critical or unexpected queries.

Openness & Transparency

All interviews will be conducted openly; no closed-door evaluations will take place. Regarding the release of interview recordings, the decision will be made through an internal vote among the current OBs, contingent upon obtaining explicit consent from every individual present during the interview.

TRACK B

The Talk

The talk track exists to serve candidates, particularly those in their first or second year, who wish to demonstrate their capabilities through a substantive public presentation rather than a conventional interview. A well-delivered talk simultaneously showcases public speaking and communication abilities, capacity to handle pressure and audience scrutiny, depth of domain knowledge in a chosen area, and organizational and preparatory discipline.

Specifications

DURATION 15 to 30 minutes
TOPIC    Free choice; must incorporate a meaningful computational or design component
FORMAT  Slides, live demos, code walkthroughs all encouraged

Acceptable domains include, but are not limited to, astrocomputing, bioinformatics, computational physics, algorithm design, web development, systems programming, UI/UX philosophy, or any other field where computation plays a non-trivial role.

⚠  Giving a talk is not a mandatory prerequisite for OB candidacy. It serves as an alternative evaluation pathway and, in effect, substitutes for the interview with equivalent weight in the final evaluation.

05

Manifesto & Election

Upon completion of all candidate evaluations, whether through Track A or Track B, candidates will proceed to the election stage. Every candidate will be required to present a manifesto detailing their vision, proposed initiatives, and concrete plans for Slashdot during the upcoming session.

The specific voterbase and the detailed voting mechanism for the election will be finalized and communicated separately in due course.

Voting results and all associated aggregate data will be made publicly available following the conclusion of the process.

Roadmap

CANDIDACY PHASE

Candidates are confirmed and assigned to their preferred track: Interview or Talk.

EVALUATION PHASE

Track A candidates are scheduled for interview slots. Track B candidates are scheduled for talk slots. All sessions conducted openly.

ELECTION PHASE

Candidates present their manifestos. Voting mechanism and voterbase to be communicated separately.

INTERNAL REVIEW

Internal vote on recording release, contingent upon consent of all participants.

15 APRIL

Final results declared. Voting data released publicly.
07

Closing Note

The spirit of this process is not exclusion but responsible stewardship. IISER Kolkata's programming and design community is growing, and we are seeing increasing engagement from students across batches and disciplines. It is precisely because of this growth that the next set of OBs must possess not only enthusiasm but also the technical grounding and organizational vision to sustain and expand the club's activities. This proposal attempts to institutionalize a selection mechanism that is transparent, fair, and cognizant of the competencies the role genuinely requires.
